Overview

Using agents is a skill

AI agents now do much more than answer questions in a chat window. They plan multi-step tasks, call tools, write and run code, search and read the literature, and work inside real software environments. Getting good results from them depends on how you frame a task, what context you provide, how you steer a longer workflow, and how carefully you check what comes back.

The first three sessions are practical: how agents work, how to give them tasks they can complete reliably, how to spot common failure modes and verify outputs, and how to build agents into everyday research and technical work. We also look at how academic work is changing, with an emphasis on using agents as research tools while keeping human verification and responsibility for the final work.

The fourth session goes behind the interface: how LLMs and agents are trained, and where AI safety questions arise. The course ends in January with a session built around what participants built, what worked, and what failed.

Learning goals

By the end of the course you should be able to…

  1. Explain what modern LLM-based AI agents are and how they differ from chat-based use of LLMs.
  2. Use agents effectively for multi-step, tool-assisted tasks.
  3. Structure tasks, instructions, context, and workflows so that agents work more reliably.
  4. Recognise common limitations and failure modes, and verify agent outputs appropriately.
  5. Integrate agents into your own research, programming, or knowledge-work workflows.
  6. Use agents for literature exploration, research organisation, and scientific writing while keeping appropriate human verification.
  7. Understand, at a high level, how LLMs and agents are trained and post-trained.
  8. Find entry points and resources for further study or research in LLMs, agents, and AI safety.

Practical info

Before you come

Who it is for

Researchers and students at CWI who want to use agents in their own work. No machine learning or LLM research background is needed for the first three sessions. Basic familiarity with AI assistants helps but is not required.

What you need

A laptop and your own subscription to an AI assistant with agent capabilities, such as Claude Code, ChatGPT Codex, or Gemini. Free versions are often too limited for the longer exercises.

Format

Five two-hour sessions. Each mixes conceptual material, practical examples, and about 30 minutes of exercises, demonstrations, or discussion. You are encouraged to experiment with agents between sessions.

The technical session

The 15 December session covers more technical material but stays accessible without an ML background. Its aim is a conceptual foundation and a roadmap for further study.
